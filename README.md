# GRAICE Framework

## About the Global Council for Responsible AI

The Global Council for Responsible AI (GCRAI) is an international body dedicated to the responsible development, governance, and adoption of artificial intelligence. Its vision is to be the world's trusted body of governance for Responsible AI, ensuring that artificial intelligence advances human dignity, security, and societal well-being, globally and at scale.

GCRAI's mission is to lead and enable responsible AI worldwide, ensuring AI systems are ethical, secure, transparent, and aligned with human values. It works to operationalize Responsible AI through global standards, certification, governance, and leadership, turning responsibility into action. Launched at the House of Lords in February 2025, the council now spans 77 country chapters and roughly 1,000 Global Ambassadors.

## About the GRAICE Framework

The Global Framework for Responsible Artificial Intelligence (GRAICE™) is GCRAI's flagship standard, launched at the World Economic Forum in Davos. It is a principle-based, operational framework for the design, development, deployment, and oversight of AI systems, and it is sector-neutral, technology-neutral, and jurisdiction-independent.

GRAICE is a meta-framework. It does not replace the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, UNESCO guidance, or the OECD AI Principles. It sits above them and provides a common, globally interoperable governance structure so that organizations can demonstrate responsible AI practice with evidence, not just principles.

The framework is built for all sectors: private and public organizations, governments and regulators, academia and research, civil society and global institutions, and enterprises from startups to multinationals.

This repository holds the public GRAICE Framework documents.

## How the framework is organized

**Six foundational values** set the ethical and human-centered boundaries: human agency and oversight, accountability and governance, transparency and explainability, fairness and impact management, reliability and security, and inclusivity and societal consideration.

**Seven pillars** translate those values into operational requirements:

1. Ethical Leadership
2. Purpose-Driven Innovation
3. Human-Centric Use
4. Responsible Implementation
5. AI Literacy and Workforce Readiness
6. Data Governance and Integrity
7. Transparency and Auditability

**Three assurance tiers** provide increasing depth of evidence:

| Tier | Name | Scope |
|------|------|-------|
| 1 | Responsible AI Readiness | Organizational governance assurance. The gateway tier. |
| 2 | System-Level Assurance | Comprehensive internal verification of a specific AI system. |
| 3 | Independent Assessment | External verification and certification. |

Together these form a continuous trust loop: governance, purpose, execution, assurance, and continuous improvement.

## Getting started

The deck's closing section outlines a three-step adoption path:

1. **Build the governance foundation.** Adopt the Responsible AI Charter, assign roles such as Responsible AI Lead and System Owners, and complete Tier 1 organizational readiness.
2. **Operationalize the seven pillars.** Integrate each pillar into workflows, create system cards, purpose statements, and data lineage, and develop training and literacy programs.
3. **Prepare for assurance.** Conduct Tier 2 internal system-level reviews, compile documentation and evidence, and consider Tier 3 independent assessment.
